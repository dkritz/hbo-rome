# Rome

## Show summary

*Rome* is a historical drama about the collapse of the Roman Republic and the violent emergence of rule by one man. Across two seasons, it follows Julius Caesar's ascent, his assassination, and the struggle that makes his adopted heir Octavian Rome's dominant ruler. These upheavals unfold through the intertwined lives of Lucius Vorenus, a rigidly honorable soldier, and Titus Pullo, his impulsive, fiercely loyal comrade. Their friendship connects battles and senatorial conspiracies to crowded tenements, criminal gangs, slavery, marriage, and religious devotion. Around them, ambitious aristocrats turn intimacy into political leverage: Atia protects her family, Servilia pursues vengeance, and Cleopatra fights for her dynasty. The series presents power as both public spectacle and private bargaining, with affection, status, and survival constantly in conflict. Its Rome is vivid, brutal, deeply religious, and morally unfamiliar, where ordinary lives repeatedly alter the destinies of people who believe themselves masters of history and their world.

## Season summaries

**Full-series spoilers follow.** These summaries describe the television drama, including its fictionalized events and relationships.

### Season 1

Julius Caesar's conquest of Gaul has made him wealthy, popular, and dangerous to Rome's governing aristocracy. His former ally Pompey increasingly sides with senators who fear Caesar's ambitions. Amid this gathering crisis, centurion Lucius Vorenus and legionary Titus Pullo recover a stolen legionary eagle and rescue Caesar's young relative Octavian. Their unlikely partnership brings them into the orbit of the powerful while exposing their sharply different temperaments: Vorenus prizes discipline and duty; Pullo follows appetite, instinct, and personal loyalty.

When political intimidation and street violence destroy hopes of compromise, Caesar crosses the Rubicon and advances into Italy. Pompey abandons Rome, leaving Caesar to occupy the capital and consolidate his position. Vorenus returns to his wife Niobe after years away, struggling to rebuild their marriage and support their household. Unknown to him, Niobe has borne another man's child. Pullo and Octavian uncover the secret and conceal it, believing that disclosure would destroy the family.

In aristocratic households, political calculation is inseparable from love and humiliation. Caesar's niece Atia manipulates her children Octavia and Octavian to secure the family's future. Her rivalry with Servilia, Caesar's lover and Brutus's mother, becomes increasingly vicious. When Caesar abandons Servilia, private heartbreak feeds a campaign of revenge. Octavia becomes entangled in this feud, while Octavian develops the intelligence and emotional detachment that will define his political career.

Civil war carries Caesar and his soldiers through Greece and Egypt. Victory at Pharsalus breaks Pompey's cause, and Pompey's murder in Egypt denies Caesar a reconciliation with his rival. Caesar supports Cleopatra's claim to the Egyptian throne. Vorenus and Pullo help deliver her to him, and Pullo secretly fathers the child publicly presented as Caesar's son, Caesarion. Returning victorious, Caesar rewards Vorenus with advancement, drawing the republican soldier into the regime he once distrusted.

Pullo fares worse in civilian life. Jealous violence destroys his hopes with the enslaved Eirene, and work as a hired killer leads to condemnation in the arena. Vorenus rescues him, making both men popular heroes. Meanwhile, Caesar's accumulating honors convince Brutus and other senators that the Republic cannot survive his rule. Servilia helps remove Vorenus from Caesar's protection by revealing Niobe's secret. He rushes home, where Niobe dies by suicide during their confrontation. At the Senate, the conspirators surround and stab Caesar. The season closes with political assassination and domestic catastrophe unfolding together, while Pullo and Eirene briefly glimpse a gentler future beyond the violence that has shaped their lives.

### Season 2

Caesar's assassination leaves Rome without a settled authority. Mark Antony survives, negotiates with the conspirators, and turns Caesar's funeral into a political victory. Yet Caesar's will names Octavian his adopted son and principal heir, creating a rival Antony initially dismisses. Octavian demands his inheritance, courts the people with Caesar's promised payments, and eventually raises an army. Atia's attachment to Antony divides her family as her son becomes increasingly independent, calculating, and ruthless.

Vorenus, shattered by Niobe's death, curses his children and believes they have been murdered by the gangster Erastes Fulmen. After killing Erastes, he sinks into despair. Antony restores his purpose by ordering him to control the Aventine's criminal collegia. Vorenus establishes authority through terror, alienating Pullo and provoking a gang war. When Pullo discovers the children are alive in slavery, he finds Vorenus with Antony's defeated army. Together they rescue the children, but returning home cannot undo their trauma or their hatred of their father.

Octavian defeats Antony, secures the consulship, and challenges Caesar's assassins. When Brutus and Cassius threaten from the east, he reconciles with Antony and Lepidus. Their alliance finances war through confiscations and organized killings, including Cicero's murder by Pullo. Victory at Philippi destroys Brutus and Cassius. The victors divide Rome's territories, but their partnership remains unstable. Servilia dies cursing Atia, and Octavian binds Antony to his family through marriage to Octavia, sacrificing both Atia's hopes and Octavia's love for Agrippa.

Private betrayals mirror the rulers' bargains. Gaia poisons Pullo's pregnant wife Eirene, killing her and their child. Vorenus discovers that his eldest daughter has supplied information to his enemies, helping them steal a shipment of gold. Devastated, he leaves Rome to serve Antony in Egypt, while Pullo takes responsibility for the Aventine and the children. Years later, Gaia's confession destroys her relationship with Pullo, who kills her.

In Alexandria, Antony's bond with Cleopatra becomes politically disastrous. Withheld grain shipments intensify Rome's hunger, while Octavian exploits Antony's rejection of Octavia and Atia to win support for war. Defeat at Actium leaves Antony and Cleopatra trapped. Both die by suicide as Octavian secures Egypt. Vorenus helps Caesarion escape, and Pullo joins them, protecting his own son from execution. Mortally wounded during their flight, Vorenus returns to his children. Octavian celebrates supremacy, but Atia finds victory hollow. Pullo falsely reports Caesarion dead, then begins telling the boy the truth about his father, preserving a private future outside the new ruler's official history.

## Character guide

### Soldiers, family, and the Aventine

| Character | Role and relationships |
| --- | --- |
| **Lucius Vorenus** | A disciplined soldier whose republican convictions collide with service to Caesar and Antony. His love of family is undermined by pride, rage, and an inflexible sense of honor. He later commands the Aventine collegium. |
| **Titus Pullo** | Vorenus's comrade and closest friend: impulsive, violent, generous, and deeply loyal. His connections to Octavian and his secret fatherhood of Caesarion link ordinary Roman life to dynastic politics. |
| **Niobe** | Vorenus's wife, who has struggled to maintain their household during his long absence. Her concealed relationship with Evander and the birth of their son drive the family's tragedy. |
| **Vorena the Elder** | Vorenus and Niobe's eldest daughter. Enslavement and sexual exploitation harden her resentment of her father; a relationship manipulated by Memmio draws her into betraying him. |
| **Vorena the Younger** | Their younger daughter, traumatized by the family's destruction. She eventually becomes a capable presence in the household and tavern under Pullo's protection. |
| **Lucius** | Niobe's son by Evander, initially presented as Vorena the Elder's child. Vorenus eventually accepts him into the family; he later apprentices to a stonemason. |
| **Lyde** | Niobe's sister and Evander's wife. Despite her own betrayal and suffering, she protects the children and becomes associated with the temple of Orbona. |
| **Eirene** | An enslaved woman whom Pullo frees and marries after killing her intended husband. Their fragile domestic happiness ends when Gaia poisons her during pregnancy. |
| **Gaia** | An enslaved brothel supervisor whose strength and ambition make her influential within the collegium. Her pursuit of Pullo leads to Eirene's murder and eventually her own death. |
| **Mascius** | A former soldier who joins Vorenus and Pullo in the Aventine's criminal business. His resentment at remaining third in command makes him a suspect when their gold is stolen. |
| **Erastes Fulmen** | A powerful local gangster whose dealings with Vorenus turn deadly. His death creates the struggle for control of the Aventine. |
| **Memmio** | A rival gang captain who uses negotiation, deception, and Vorena's relationship with one of his men against Vorenus. Pullo ultimately defeats and imprisons him. |

### Rome's ruling households and rivals

| Character | Role and relationships |
| --- | --- |
| **Julius Caesar** | Victorious general, political reformer, and increasingly dominant ruler. His generosity and ambition win devotion while convincing opponents that he threatens the Republic. |
| **Pompey Magnus** | Caesar's former ally and eventual military opponent. His prestige cannot prevent defeat, flight, and assassination in Egypt. |
| **Mark Antony** | Caesar's charismatic, brutal lieutenant and Atia's lover. His rivalry with Octavian and alliance with Cleopatra lead from power sharing to civil war and defeat. |
| **Octavian / Octavian Caesar** | Atia's son and Caesar's adopted heir. He grows from an observant adolescent into a calculating ruler who subordinates friendship and family to political control. |
| **Atia of the Julii** | Caesar's niece and mother of Octavian and Octavia. An aggressive family strategist whose love for Antony exposes vulnerabilities that political success cannot heal. |
| **Octavia** | Atia's daughter, repeatedly used to advance family interests. Her love for Agrippa conflicts with the political marriage that binds her to Antony. |
| **Servilia of the Junii** | Caesar's former lover and Brutus's mother. Her feud with Atia and desire for vengeance intertwine with the conspiracy against Caesar. |
| **Marcus Junius Brutus** | Torn between affection for Caesar, family expectations, and republican ideals. He joins the assassination and later dies after defeat at Philippi. |
| **Gaius Cassius Longinus** | Brutus's fellow conspirator and military partner, more openly committed to resistance against Caesar and his successors. |
| **Cicero** | An influential senator and orator who tries to preserve senatorial authority through shifting alliances. His attempt to use Octavian against Antony ends in his murder. |
| **Cato and Scipio** | Prominent republican opponents of Caesar who continue resistance after Pompey's defeat and die as that resistance collapses in Africa. |
| **Marcus Lepidus** | The third partner in the ruling triumvirate, overshadowed by Antony and Octavian and assigned Africa in their division of territory. |
| **Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa** | Octavian's trusted soldier and adviser, and Octavia's lover. His conscience repeatedly conflicts with loyalty to the man whose victories he helps secure. |
| **Maecenas** | Octavian's sophisticated, pleasure-loving adviser, adept at money, information, and political maneuvering. |
| **Livia** | Octavian's calculating new wife, who understands his methods and expects influence within his household. Her arrival creates a new rival for Atia. |
| **Posca** | Caesar's enslaved secretary, freed by his will, who subsequently serves Antony. His knowledge and eventual defection give Octavian crucial political ammunition. |
| **Jocasta** | Octavia's friend, whose family is destroyed in the proscriptions. She marries Posca and later escapes Egypt with him. |
| **Calpurnia** | Caesar's wife, whose household becomes the setting for the reading of his politically explosive will. |
| **Timon** | Atia's Jewish fixer and occasional lover. Increasingly troubled by the violence he performs, he struggles with his brother Levi's militancy and eventually leaves Rome with his family. |
| **Levi** | Timon's brother, whose religious and political anger leads to a plan to assassinate Herod. Their disagreement ends in his death. |

### Egypt and Judea

| Character | Role and relationships |
| --- | --- |
| **Cleopatra** | Egypt's queen, who uses alliances with Caesar and Antony to preserve her throne and dynasty. Her struggle for survival ends when she recognizes Octavian's intentions. |
| **Caesarion** | Publicly Caesar's son and Cleopatra's heir, but privately Pullo's child. His claimed ancestry makes him a threat to Octavian and the focus of the final escape. |
| **Ptolemy XIII** | Cleopatra's brother and rival for Egypt's throne, whose regime misjudges Caesar by presenting him with Pompey's severed head. |
| **Helios and Selene** | Antony and Cleopatra's children. After their parents' deaths, Octavian brings them to Rome, where Octavia receives them. |
| **Herod** | A client ruler seeking Roman support for his claim to Judea. His enormous payment to Antony fuels disputes among the triumvirs and the theft plot on the Aventine. |
